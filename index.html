NCERT Topic 5.1 & 5.1.1

The Polynucleotide Chain & Double Helix

A polymer of deoxyribonucleotides. Watson & Crick (1953) proposed the double helix model based on X-ray diffraction data by Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin.

01

Three Core Components

Every single nucleotide is constructed from three distinct sub-units:

  • 1. Nitrogenous Base: Purines (Adenine & Guanine, 9-membered double ring) and Pyrimidines (Cytosine & Thymine/Uracil, 6-membered single ring).
  • 2. Pentose Sugar: Ribose in RNA and 2'-deoxyribose in DNA (lacks oxygen at the 2'-carbon position).
  • 3. Phosphate Group: Esterified to 5'-OH of a nucleoside, imparts net negative charge to DNA.
02

The Vital Chemical Bonds

Frequent CBSE Board 3-mark & 5-mark question area:

N-Glycosidic Linkage

Connects nitrogenous base to the 1'-C of pentose sugar to form a Nucleoside.

Phosphodiester Bond

Links 3'-OH of one nucleotide to 5'-phosphate of adjacent nucleotide, creating the continuous backbone.

Hydrogen Bonds

Connects bases: 2 H-bonds between A & T; 3 H-bonds between G & C. Conveys helical stability together with plane-stacking.

03

Salient Double Helix Traits

NCERT canonical criteria for the Watson-Crick B-DNA structure:

  • Two polynucleotide chains with antiparallel polarity (5'→3' & 3'→5').
  • Backbone = Sugar-Phosphate; Bases project inside.
  • Coiled in a right-handed helical fashion.
  • Pitch = 3.4 nm; roughly 10 bp per turn; distance between bp = 0.34 nm.
  • Stacking of one base pair over another confers stability.

Erwin Chargaff's Equivalence Rule

In double-stranded DNA, the ratios between Adenine & Thymine and Guanine & Cytosine are constant and equal to one. [A] = [T] and [G] = [C] , hence [A + G] = [T + C] (Purines = Pyrimidines).

T 30%
G 20%

Total = 100% | Purines (50%) = Pyrimidines (50%)

Streptococcus pneumoniae

Griffith's Transforming Principle

Frederick Griffith performed experiments with Streptococcus pneumoniae (Pneumococcus). He observed two strains:

S-Strain (Smooth)

Mucous (polysaccharide) coat present. Virulent / Pathogenic. Mice die of pneumonia.

R-Strain (Rough)

No mucous coat. Non-virulent. Mice survive and remain healthy.

Option 1 Selected
Active Injection: Living R-Strain (Non-virulent)
🐁
Mouse Alive & Healthy

R-strain (rough) lacks the protective polysaccharide coat. It is non-virulent and easily cleared by the host immune system.

💡 Griffith's Deduction: Chemical factor transferred to R cells transforming them into virulent S cells.

Meselson & Stahl (1958) • CsCl Density Gradient

Semiconservative DNA Replication

Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl grew E. coli in medium containing heavy isotope ¹⁵NH₄Cl, then transferred them to normal light medium ¹⁴NH₄Cl.

Taylor & colleagues (1958): Proved semiconservative replication in chromosomes of Vicia faba (faba beans) using radioactive thymidine.

Francis Crick's Principle

The Central Dogma of Molecular Biology

Unidirectional flow of genetic information: DNA → mRNA → Protein (Reverse transcription in retroviruses: Temin & Baltimore).

The Replication Machinery & Enzymes

Replication is catalyzed with astonishing accuracy by DNA-dependent DNA polymerase (~2000 bp/sec in E. coli). Polymerization proceeds strictly in 5' → 3' direction.

Continuous / Leading Strand: Template with polarity 3' → 5'. Synthesis is continuous towards replication fork.
Discontinuous / Lagging Strand (Okazaki Fragments): Template with polarity 5' → 3'. Synthesized as fragments, later joined seamlessly by DNA Ligase.
Deoxyribonucleoside Triphosphates (dNTPs): Serve dual purpose: 1) Act as substrates, 2) Provide energy for polymerization from terminal phosphates.

Transcription Unit & Eukaryotic Splicing

The process of copying genetic information from one strand of DNA into RNA. Governed by the principle of complementarity, except Uracil binds to Adenine instead of Thymine.

3 Components of a Transcription Unit: 1. Promoter (binding site for RNA Pol, upstream at 5' end of coding strand). 2. Structural gene. 3. Terminator (defines end of transcription at 3' end).
Template vs Coding Strand Rule: Strand with 3' → 5' polarity acts as Template. The other strand with 5' → 3' polarity is called Coding Strand (all promoter reference coordinates use Coding strand).
Eukaryotic Processing (hnRNA → mRNA): Capping: Methyl guanosine triphosphate added to 5'-end. Tailing: Poly-A tail (200-300 adenylate residues) added at 3'-end. Splicing: Introns (non-coding) removed, exons (coding) ligated.

The Genetic Code: George Gamow & Nirenberg

4 nitrogenous bases create 4³ = 64 triplet codons, coding for 20 proteinogenic amino acids.

  • • Triplet Code: 61 codons code for amino acids; 3 codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) are Stop/Nonsense codons.
  • • Unambiguous & Specific: One codon codes for only one amino acid.
  • • Degenerate: Some amino acids are specified by more than one codon.
  • • Universal: From bacteria to humans, UUU codes for Phenylalanine.
  • • Dual Role of AUG: 1) Codes for Methionine (Met), 2) Acts as the Initiator/Start codon.

Translation & The tRNA Adapter Molecule

Translation is the process of polymerizing amino acids into a polypeptide chain directed by mRNA sequences.

Charging of tRNA (Aminoacylation): Amino acids are activated in the presence of ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA (catalyzed by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase).
tRNA 2D & 3D Structure: Secondary structure resembles a Cloverleaf; actual 3D structure looks like a compact inverted L. Contains an anticodon loop that reads mRNA in antiparallel orientation.
Ribosome as Ribozyme: In bacteria, 23S rRNA acts as the catalyst (ribozyme) for peptide bond formation between P and A sites.
Stages of Translation:
1
Initiation Ribosome small subunit binds mRNA at start codon (AUG). Initiator tRNA brings Met to P site.
2
Elongation New aminoacyl-tRNA binds A site; peptide bond forms; ribosome translocates codon by codon towards 3'-end.
3
Termination Release Factor binds to Stop Codon (UAA/UAG/UGA); polypeptide chain released, ribosome dissociates.

2. Digestion with Restriction Endonucleases
3. Separation by Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

Quick Revision Digest

CBSE Master Revision Sheet

Every high-weightage data point from NCERT Chapter 5 condensed for quick examination recall.

🧬 Human Genome Project (HGP)

  • • 13-year mega project coordinated by US Dept of Energy and NIH (1990 - 2003).
  • • Human genome has 3.1647 × 10⁹ base pairs.
  • • Total genes = ~30,000 (avg gene has 3000 bases; Dystrophin is largest with 2.4 million bases).
  • • Chromosome 1 has highest genes (2968); Y has lowest (231).
  • • Less than 2% of the genome codes for proteins.
  • • Approx 1.4 million locations where single-base DNA differences (SNPs) occur.

⚖️ DNA vs RNA as Genetic Material

  • • Why DNA is superior for storage: 2'-OH in ribose makes RNA labile and easily degradable; DNA lacks 2'-OH.
  • • Presence of Thymine (5-methyl uracil) confers additional stability over Uracil.
  • • Double stranded nature and repair mechanisms prevent excessive mutation.
  • • RNA is better for direct protein transmission; exhibits catalytic properties (Ribozymes).

⚙️ The 3 Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases

  • • RNA Polymerase I: Transcribes 28S, 18S, and 5.8S rRNAs.
  • • RNA Polymerase II: Transcribes precursor of mRNA, called heterogeneous nuclear RNA (hnRNA).
  • • RNA Polymerase III: Transcribes tRNA, 5S rRNA, and snRNAs (small nuclear RNAs).
